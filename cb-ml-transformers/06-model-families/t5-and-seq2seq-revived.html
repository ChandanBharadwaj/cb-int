T5 & Seq2Seq Revived

The third family closes the triangle. T5 (2019) resurrected M4.3's encoder-decoder shape with transformer guts and one radical framing — every NLP task is text-to-text — then ran the field's most systematic bake-off of pretraining choices and left two artifacts inside every tool you use: the C4 corpus and the everything-is-a-prompt interface. Its descendants still quietly run the translation and speech systems in your pocket, and its pretraining objective completes a set: after this page you hold all three families with their native objectives, unified around one example sentence.

Everything is text-to-text

Span corruption, worked end to end

T5's pretraining objective sits between BERT's and GPT's, and one worked example nails it. Take "Thank you for inviting me to your party last week". Corrupt it: pick random spans (mean length ~3 tokens, ~15% of the text), collapse each into a single sentinel token. The encoder reads the corrupted stream: "Thank you <X> me to your party <Y> week". The decoder must generate only the missing material, tagged by sentinel: "<X> for inviting <Y> last <Z>". Notice the three design wins packed in there: spans are harder than isolated blanks (the model must produce "for inviting" as connected text, not two independent guesses); the targets are generated autoregressively, so the decoder gets properly trained, unlike BERT's in-place slot-filling; and the output is far shorter than the input — a compute win, since decoder passes only pay for the missing 15%.

Three objectives, one sentence

Now line up all three families on that same sentence, because this comparison is the module in a single view. MLM (BERT): blank out "for" and "last", predict each in place from both sides — needs an encoder, trains understanding, can't generate. Causal LM (GPT): no corruption at all — predict every next word left to right — needs a masked decoder, trains generation, 100% signal density. Span corruption (T5): collapse spans, regenerate them in sequence — wants both towers: bidirectional reading of what survives, autoregressive writing of what's missing. Objective and architecture pair up; none of the three is "best" — each is the native game of its silhouette. And one hybrid worth naming because it resurfaced: the prefix LM — bidirectional attention over a prompt region, causal over the continuation — a T5-paper variant that reappears in modern multimodal models (image tokens read bidirectionally, text generated causally).

The great bake-off

The T5 paper's larger contribution was methodological: it systematically compared objectives (LM vs MLM vs span corruption), architectures, data sizes and fine-tuning schemes under one controlled budget — the field's most complete ablation study of its era. Two conclusions to carry: denoising objectives beat plain LM for downstream fine-tuning at that scale, and most clever variations mattered less than scale and data quality — an early rhyme of M7.4's scaling laws, published two years before them.

Where enc-dec survives, and why decoders still won

Encoder-decoder remains the right tool where the input deserves full bidirectional reading and the output is a faithful transform: translation (production systems), Whisper (audio encoder → text decoder), BART-style summarization, and — a fact worth knowing — Flan-T5 remained the best instruction-following model per parameter for years. But the general race went decoder-only (M5.8's four reasons: universality, objective simplicity, in-context learning, serving economics). T5's text-to-text vision won culturally even as its architecture lost the scale race: "everything is a prompt, every answer is text" is the modern interface.

Real numbers

T5 sizes: Small 60M → Base 220M (≈ BERT-base + GPT-2-small stapled together) → XXL 11B. Trained on C4 — 750GB of cleaned CommonCrawl the paper open-sourced, which became the seed of many later corpora (FineWeb, from M6.1's build, is a descendant). Whisper, the enc-dec you've most likely used this week: 1.5B params, 680k hours of audio — cross-attention (M5.8) letting text tokens attend over audio frames. And the instruction-tuning preview hiding in this family: the Flan collection fine-tuned T5 on ~1,800+ tasks phrased as instructions — producing, in Flan-T5, the best instruction-follower per parameter of its era and the proof-of-concept M8.3 industrialized.

Connect the dots

T5's fingerprints on your daily tools: the ▁-prefixed tokens you may have seen in HF tokenizer dumps are its SentencePiece legacy (M5.1); "instruction: … output: …" dataset formats descend from its task prefixes; and if your product ever transcribes audio, cross-attention from this family is doing the work. Build: drive T5's prefixes yourself
from transformers import pipeline

t5 = pipeline("text2text-generation", model="google/flan-t5-base")

for prompt in [
    "Translate English to German: The house is wonderful.",
    "Summarize: The transformer architecture, introduced in 2017, replaced "
    "recurrent networks by using attention to process all tokens in parallel, "
    "enabling much larger models and faster training on modern hardware.",
    "Is this sentence grammatical? 'The car drove quickly street.'",
    "Answer: What is the capital of Karnataka?",
]:
    print("\n>", prompt[:60], "...")
    print(" ", t5(prompt, max_new_tokens=40)[0]["generated_text"])

Flan-T5-base: 220M parameters — 1/800th of GPT-3 — following instructions credibly on CPU. Worth experiencing to calibrate "how much model does my task actually need?", a question M8.6 turns into a decision framework.

If you remember one thing

T5 = the M5.8 encoder-decoder + span-corruption pretraining + one big idea: every task is text-to-text. The architecture now specializes (translation, Whisper); the idea became the industry's interface. And its bake-off left the receipts: objectives matter some, scale and data matter more.

Interview questions

What is span corruption and how does it differ from BERT's MLM?

Contiguous spans (mean length ~3) are replaced by single sentinel tokens, and the decoder generates the concatenated missing spans autoregressively. Differences from MLM: targets are generated as a sequence (training the decoder properly), spans are harder than isolated tokens (more context integration), and the output is shorter than the input (efficient). It's a denoising objective adapted to the enc-dec shape — same family as BART's corruptions.

What did the T5 paper's systematic study conclude, roughly?

Under controlled budgets: denoising/span objectives beat plain LM for fine-tuned downstream quality; encoder-decoder with shared vocab was strong for text-to-text; most architectural/objective tweaks yielded marginal gains compared to scaling model, data and training — foreshadowing scaling laws. Its enduring artifacts: the C4 corpus and the text-to-text framing. Citing "T5's ablations showed recipe < scale" is a compact, credible interview move.

What is a prefix LM and where does the idea appear today?

An attention pattern splitting the sequence in two regimes: the prefix (prompt/condition) gets full bidirectional attention among its own tokens, while the continuation is generated causally, attending to the prefix and its own past. It sits between encoder-decoder (two towers) and decoder-only (all causal) — one tower, two masks. T5's paper studied it as an objective variant; today the shape recurs wherever a fully-available condition feeds generation in one stack: multimodal models reading image tokens bidirectionally before generating text causally, and some code/infill models treating surrounding context as a bidirectional prefix. Naming it marks you as knowing the mask (M5.7) is a design surface, not a binary.

Sketch the input and target streams for span corruption on a sentence of your choice.

Example — original: "the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog". Choose spans: "quick brown" and "the lazy". Input stream (encoder): "the <X> fox jumps over <Y> dog". Target stream (decoder): "<X> quick brown <Y> the lazy <Z>" — sentinels tag which gap each span fills, ordered left to right, with a final sentinel closing the sequence. Grading points: spans (not single tokens) collapsed to ONE sentinel each; target contains only the removed material plus sentinels; a terminal sentinel ends the target. Being able to produce this cold demonstrates you understand the objective rather than its name.

Why does Whisper use an encoder-decoder rather than decoder-only?

The input (audio) is a complete, fixed signal deserving full bidirectional encoding — there's no causality constraint on listening. The decoder then generates text attending over those audio representations via cross-attention. Decoder-only would force audio through the same causal stream as text, wasting the ability to hear the whole utterance before writing. General rule: when input and output are different modalities or the input is fully available upfront, enc-dec is natural.

Did T5's "text-to-text" idea win or lose?

Both, instructively: the architecture lost the general-purpose race to decoder-only (M5.8's economics), but the interface won completely — modern LLM usage ("instruction in, text out", classification answered as words, numbers as digits) is T5's framing executed by a different architecture. Flan-T5 also proved instruction tuning's power per parameter before it was standard. Good answer-shape for interviews: separate the fate of the idea from the fate of the implementation.
